A recall tolerance for the Balasore evaluation set therefore cannot be justified from heaping in that sample; it has to come from somewhere else.

## 8. Where these numbers differ from the 23 Nov review

Reproduced to the decimal: all seven transplanted-only transplanting SDs, the robust 10.4 d for Odisha 2017, the 22.9 d all-method Odisha 2017 figure and its 35.7% direct-seeded share, the 9.9%/0% direct-seeded shares for Odisha 2018 and Balasore, the three harvest SDs (18.3 / 12.8 / 16.9), Balasore duration SD 7.4, the 118 co-located cells with up to 7 farmers, all 185 Balasore points inside the registry bbox, and Bihar 2017's harvest MAE pair (11.30 → 9.26 against the review's 11.3 → 9.4). Five differ:

1. **2018 row count: 8,355, not 8,393.** 8,393 is the file's line count; 38 records contain embedded newlines, so the parsed row count is lower. Every district count the review gave is right (they sum to the 784 Odisha rows we see).
2. **Balasore villages: 36 distinct names, not 30.** 30 is the number with ≥ 3 farmers, which is the subset §4 uses.
3. **Balasore MTU 7029: n = 124, duration SD 5.62 d** here, against the review's n = 120 / 5.7 d. The review counted the literal string `MTU7029`; `normalise_variety` also folds in 4 rows of `Mansuri_MTU7029` and friends. On the literal string alone we get n = 120, SD **5.67 d** — the review's number exactly.
4. **Within-village SD (§4) depends on the definition.** The review's 9.0 d for Odisha 2018 is the *pooled* within-village SD (we measure 9.04); the mean of per-village SDs is **5.73 d**. Its 11.8 d for Balasore we cannot reproduce under either definition (we get 5.64 mean / 13.01 pooled), and the pooled figure is dominated by one row — see 5.
5. **Odisha 2017 harvest MAE: 13.49 → 10.42 d here, against the review's 19.8 → 17.1.** The review's pair is reproducible only on *uncleaned* rows: all establishment methods and no plausibility filter gives 17.16 → 14.60, and dropping the variety n ≥ 5 requirement pushes the baseline to 21.6. Since the method agrees exactly on Bihar 2017 (99.5% transplanted, almost nothing dropped), the gap is which Odisha rows are in, not how the MAE is computed. **The conclusion is unchanged in every version: transplanting + per-variety duration beats the median-harvest baseline.**

## 9. The finding that was not in the review

**Balasore's raw transplanting SD of 12.7 d is one field.** Dropping the single out-of-season row — village Kasida, transplanted 10 Feb 2018, harvested 13 May 2018, 92 days, which is a *summer (boro) rice* crop and not kharif at all — takes the SD from **12.68 d to 5.70 d** (n = 184), with a robust scale of **4.45 d** and a within-village spread of 3.77 d. The same single-row sensitivity runs through Balasore's harvest SD (16.88 → 6.42 d) and the pooled within-village figure (13.01 → 5.36 d). Odisha 2018 as a whole moves 11.14 → 9.39 d for the same reason.

So on this sample the kharif transplanting spread in our own study district is **about 5–6 days, roughly half of SICKLE's 10.0 d** — the *opposite* direction to D-031's pre-registered prediction. Three reasons not to treat that as settling anything: these are 185 farmers in 36 purposively surveyed villages, not a sample of the 133,055-field Baleswar registry; they are farmer recall, which rounds (§7) and is not what a radar flooding-date detector would measure; and 2018 is one monsoon. It is disclosed prior evidence that cuts against the prediction, not a measurement of it (D-034).
